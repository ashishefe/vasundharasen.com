Method

What each status means, what counts as a change, how corrections work, and how each edition is made.

Statuses

Each rule carries one status, taken from the latest public record we found. A status marked Press-reported rests on press coverage because we have not found the official text.

Changes

A change is a dated public step: a notification, a draft, the opening or close of a consultation, an order, an amendment, a scheme decision, or a deadline. “This month” means dated after the previous edition. Steps from before that date which earlier editions missed are listed separately as earlier changes added in this edition. Announcements with no text, and speeches, are not counted as changes.

Corrections

When a later check shows an earlier entry was wrong, we fix the entry and add a dated correction under it saying what the earlier edition said and what the record shows. Corrections are listed on the Overview for the edition that makes them.

Editions

    How an edition is made

    1. A research agent reads the public registers: Gazette notifications where they can be reached, ministry and regulator websites, parliamentary replies and government press releases. It checks every existing entry against its source and drafts a proposal of new entries and corrections, each with its sources.
    2. A person checks the proposal and approves it. Nothing is applied without that approval.
    3. The build checks every entry: each must cite a public source, each date must be valid and each status must be one listed above. Entries we cannot source stay out of the public pages.
    4. Earlier editions’ entries are kept. A correction is shown, never made silently.
